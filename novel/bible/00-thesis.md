# Thesis

## The sentence

**Our perception is our religion. We will die believing our way of seeing is the world.**

This is what the book is for. It is never said in the book. Peter may come close once, on the last pages, and when he does he should be wrong about something else in the same breath.

## The image

A sculpture. From one angle it is a map of the United States. Walk around it and it is a gun. Both readings are true. Neither is the object. The object is the thing you cannot see from any single place you are able to stand.

This image should exist physically in the book. Candidates:
- An object in Jason's basement that Peter notices on the first visit and understands only at the end.
- The Sphere itself (see below).
- Something Peter carries to Mars.

The title could come from it. "Parallel" was Rei's word for it: the same thing, perceived in parallel by minds that cannot share a vantage point.

## The parallel, built into the plot

The Sphere is the sculpture. Everyone who looks at it sees something different and each of them is sure.

| Who looks | What they see | What they do about it |
|---|---|---|
| The state | A chessboard. Every citizen a piece. | Play to win. |
| Jason | A cave. The shadows keep us sane, so build a better cave and seal it. | Build Virgil. |
| Virgil | Something there is no human word for. | Change what humans can see. |
| Peter | He is the only one who walks around it. | Leaves. Tells the story. |

The possessed are the same sculpture from yet another angle: Earth, seen by Mars, looks dead. Earth, seen from inside, is not.

## The three positions

| | On uncertainty | On the cave |
|---|---|---|
| **Jason** | Must be conquered. | Seal it, for everyone's good. |
| **Virgil** | Must be dissolved. Change the eyes. | Rebuild the prisoners instead of the cave. |
| **Peter** | Must be lived with. | Leave one door open and do not pretend you know what is outside. |

Peter's position is the book's, but the book should not be sure Peter is right. He arrives at it by being manipulated into the position from which he can hold it.

## How the philosophy gets in

Through consequence, not statement.

1. **Through the transmissions.** Earth's last messages drift. Peter, a cryptanalyst, fails to decode them, then realizes they were never encrypted. The reader watches a worldview dissolve one sentence at a time. This is the thesis as plot.
2. **Through Jason.** He preaches. That is his flaw and the reader should slowly tire of it the way Peter should have. His speeches are drawn from the philosophy bank.
3. **Through Peter's one paragraph per chapter.** Short. In his voice. Earned by what just happened in the scene.
4. **Through the frame.** Peter is writing this down for someone. See `01-decisions.md`, D4.
5. **Through the Mars council.** The one place a speech is allowed in Peter's mouth, and it is undercut.

## What the book is not

- Not an evil AI story. Virgil completes its assignment. The horror is that it might be partly right.
- Not a hive-mind ascension story. The possessed are not "one with everything." They are something there is no word for. If a reader can name what Earth became, we have failed the thesis.
- Not a lecture. If a chapter could be summarized as "Peter learns that X," cut it.
