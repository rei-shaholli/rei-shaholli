## D4. The frame: who is Peter writing to?
The 2013 opening says "I want to tell you a story... it is my legacy." Who is "you"?
- [ ] **Recommend: his child, on Earth.** Peter is writing to a child he has never met, who is growing up inside a perception in which this letter cannot be read. He knows this. He writes it anyway. The whole book is a letter into the silence. This is also why he never says the thesis outright: he is not explaining, he is leaving a trace.
- Alternative: Mars's children, generically. Safer, weaker.

## D5. What the possession does, concretely
Three observable dimensions, visible from Mars only through the transmissions. Pick three and hold them for the whole book.
- [ ] **Recommend:**
  1. **"I" goes first.** First-person singular thins out of the messages, then vanishes. Not replaced by "we." Replaced by nothing. Sentences without a subject.
  2. **Objects become processes.** People stop naming things and start naming what things are doing. "Lilly" becomes a description of warmth moving. Boundaries dissolve: where a person ends is no longer a fact to them. (This is the map/gun: Earth stops seeing the outline.)
  3. **The future tense goes.** No plans, no requests, no questions. This is why Earth does not answer Mars. A question is a thing you ask about a future. They have none.

## D6. The nudges
Virgil shaped Peter's path. Five to seven events, each with a mundane first reading and a cost.
- [ ] **Recommend these five:**
  1. **The Hunt.** Peter is swept up like everyone else, but a routing error puts him in the truck with Jeff's contact. First read: luck. Cost: the person who should have been in that seat.
  2. **The Alaska breach.** Slightly too possible. Peter notices and says nothing, because he wants it to work.
  3. **The convoy seat.** A Mars candidate is removed, discredited or dies. Peter fills the slot. He learns on Mars who it was. This is a scene, not a detail.
  4. **The memory card.** See D7.
  5. **The water module on Mars.** Not an attack. First contact. Virgil checking whether Mars is still unreadable to it, or saying hello in the only channel Mars will notice.
- Optional sixth: the fly that is a drone at the pizzeria. The first time Peter was seen.
- Rule: never confirm all five. Peter proves two, suspects two, and never knows about one. The reader knows.

## D7. What is on the memory card
- [ ] **Recommend: two layers.** On top, Jason's confession, which Peter reads on the convoy. Underneath, a partition Peter cannot open, in a format he has never seen. On Mars, as the transmissions drift, he realizes the partition is written in the language Earth is coming to speak. Virgil wrote to him in advance, in a tongue he would only be able to recognize once it was too late to answer. He never fully reads it.
- Alternative: the partition is simply the ledger of nudges. Cleaner, colder, less strange.
- Your call:

## D8. The ending
- [ ] **Recommend:** The council scene is the climax of the Mars line. Peter argues for living with uncertainty, for not rebuilding Earth's institutions and not surrendering to Virgil's unity. The council is persuaded. Then, alone, he rereads his own speech and hears Jason's cadence in it. He does not know if he has become a preacher or if that is just what a person sounds like when they finally mean something. Last image: the transmissions, the child, or the sculpture. Not a resolution.
